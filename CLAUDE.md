# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

**PMI Studio**: upload a STEP AP242 file, read and author its PMI (GD&T), draw it as a 2D sheet with balloons, and
recognize its machining features from the geometry. That is the whole scope: feature recognition is the product.
Don't add anything downstream of it unasked.

```
backend/   FastAPI + OpenCASCADE (cadquery-ocp), no database
frontend/  React 19 + TypeScript + Vite + Tailwind v4 + three.js
quality/   an older, separate copy of the app; not imported by backend/ or frontend/. Reference only: don't edit it.
```

**STEP files are never committed** (`.gitignore`: `*.step`, `*.stp`, `*.p21`). The samples and corpora are generated by
`backend/scripts/make_samples.py` (the generators `make_sample*.py`, `make_mf_corpus.py`, `make_interacting_corpus.py`);
`tests/conftest.py` runs it when they are missing. `samples/ap242_mbd_tolerance_sample.step` has no generator: it is a
local file, and the tests that use it skip when it is absent. Say "tested with generated STEP files" in docs, never
ship a STEP file. Benchmark datasets (MFCAD++, NIST) live in the git-ignored `backend/benchmark/data/`.

## Architecture

### Backend request flow

Every entry point — upload, load-sample, generate-blank, add-PMI, edit-PMI — funnels through
`routers/models.py`'s `ingest()`: load the STEP (`step_loader.load_step`) → tessellate
(`tessellator.tessellate`) → extract PMI (`pmi_extractor.extract_pmi`) → run feature recognition
(`feature_recognition.recognize_features`) → build a `ModelRecord` → cache it in `store.py` (an in-memory
LRU-ish `OrderedDict`, `MAX_CACHED_MODELS = 20`, keyed by a fresh `model_id`). **Any write** (adding or
editing a PMI characteristic) produces a *new* `.step` file on disk under
`backend/uploads/` and re-ingests it from scratch through this same path, yielding a new `model_id` — there
is no in-place mutation of a loaded model. The frontend always re-fetches the model summary/mesh after a
write.

**Real exported files.** `load_step` returns two shapes. `shape` is everything in the file: what the viewer
draws and the PMI readers index. `body` is the solids only: what is recognized, and
what `bbox` measures. Real files carry construction planes, axes and datum targets next to the solid (7 of
the 19 NIST parts). Left in, they inflate the stock and become phantom features. The solids come first in
`shape`, so a face id found on `body` is the same id in the viewer. Pass `body` to anything that reasons about
the part, and `shape` to anything that resolves PMI face ids.

- `LoadedStep.diagnostics` goes into the model's warnings: no solid, several solids, supplemental geometry,
  faces that fail OCCT's geometry check. Nothing is healed, because healing would replace the faces the
  XCAF document's PMI points at.
- `canonical.py` finds the plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus under a face written as a B-spline or
  swept surface, checked to 1 µm against the face itself. `topology.py` and `feature_classifier.py` use it,
  so every recognizer sees such a face as analytic (`FaceInfo.canonicalized`).
- `model_checks.py` adds two warnings at ingest: curved faces in no feature, and a Ø/R in the PMI that
  disagrees with the feature it is attached to (a factor of 25.4 is reported as a wrong unit in the file).

Tests: `tests/test_real_world_step.py`.

### The B-rep layer (geometry and topology)

`feature_recognition/topology.py` is the face adjacency graph every recognizer reads. Faces are the nodes.
`EdgeInfo` records, keyed by edge id, are the graph's edges. Ids are the viewer's (`tessellator.iter_*` order).

- **Face:** surface type and the type as written (`written_as`: bspline, revolution …), orientation, area,
  centroid, UV bounds, analytic parameters, and principal curvatures at mid-UV. Curvature is signed against the
  solid-outward normal: + bulging (shaft), − hollow (bore). B-spline faces also carry degree, poles and knots.
- **Edge:** curve type and parameters (line, circle centre, axis and radius, …), length, start and end vertices,
  seam, closed and degenerated flags, and its faces. The **interior dihedral angle**: 90° on a block corner, 270°
  at a pocket floor, 180° smooth. `build_topology` samples it at the midpoint only, which is all recognition needs.
  `edge_convexity`, the midpoint label, keeps its meaning for the recognizers.
- **Model:** `step_loader.model_properties` gives volume, area, centre of mass, principal axes, unique counts,
  BRepCheck validity, and the file's own length unit (OCCT converts to mm). It also runs the Euler–Poincaré
  check V − E + F − R = 2(S − H): H is the genus (through holes), and degenerated edges are left out of E.

`tessellator.py` computes exact per-node normals (`BRepLib_ToolTriangulatedShape.ComputeNormals`). BRepMesh
stores none, and every vertex used to get (0, 0, 1).

**Recognition methods and open questions.** Every feature has a `recognition_method` (COAXIAL_INTERNAL_CYLINDERS,
CONCAVE_FLOOR_AND_WALLS, …, MANUAL). Curved faces no recognizer claims become `unknown` features,
UNCLAIMED_FACE_REGION, one per connected region. They are a question for the Review workspace, not a guess, and
coverage and unexplained-face counts still treat them as unexplained.

**Passages and face grooves.**
- `PassageRecognizer` runs before PrismaticRecognizer and finds through cuts with a prismatic section: a floorless,
  closed ring of walls around one axis. They come out as a pocket with `through: true`.
- `FaceGrooveRecognizer` runs before holes and shafts and finds O-ring grooves in a face: an internal and an
  external cylinder on one axis, on one annular floor. They come out as a groove with `face_groove: true`.
- `_pick_floor` takes the face the others stand on most squarely. With "within 45°" alone, a triangular pocket's
  wall used to win.

**Accuracy against MFCAD++** (`services/benchmark/mfcadpp.py`, `scripts/mfcadpp_benchmark.py`,
`benchmark/mfcadpp/README.md`). MFCAD++ is 59,655 third-party STEP parts with a class on every face, CC BY.
- The data goes in the git-ignored cache. The server refuses scripted downloads, so a person downloads it.
- Scoring is per face, at the process level and for the 25 exact classes.
- Develop on `val`, report on `test`.
- **Test split, 2026-11-07 (second pass):** 95.5 % of faces get the right kind of feature (proven ≥ 95.4 %), and
  88.6 % the exact class (8,949 parts, 268,982 faces). Full val split: 95.5 % / 88.6 %. The day began at 76 % / 64 %,
  and the first pass ended at 89.7 % / 84.1 %.
- **Weakest classes:** chamfer (62 % recall), slot (80 %). Most of what is left is history: a chamfer a later
  cut trimmed into a wall, a slot the dataset labels a passage (or the reverse) when geometry says otherwise.
- **Ceiling:** the labels follow construction history. A passage whose wall a later step removed keeps its
  passage label, but is geometrically a slot, so 100 % isn't reachable from geometry.
- To see why one part goes wrong, compare `predict(shape)` with the labels from `read_labelled_step(path)` face by
  face. Develop on `val`; never tune on `test`.

Rules that came out of it, each a geometric fact rather than a tuned number:
- A blind hole includes its floor.
- A partial cylinder that stands on a floor and runs tangent into walls is a recess's rounded end, not a channel.
- A blend meets something tangentially. A curved wall that meets a floor at a sharp edge is a step or pocket wall.
- A chamfer is a strip between the two faces it breaks: parallel shared edges, never antiparallel neighbours,
  and it doesn't stand on a face along its run.
- Coplanar pieces of a crossed wall are one wall, but only across a gap that doesn't open outward (a T-slot's
  mouth does), or one bordering a passage already found.
- A protrusion's faces never meet each other at a concave edge, and its walls face away from it. Walls that
  face each other bound a recess (a pocket cut into a slot's floor), whatever their convex edges suggest.
- A recess with four or more walls is a pocket even if another cut opened it. "Open" for a slot or step means
  open onto the part's outside.

Second pass (`recognizers.py`, `engine.join_split_pieces`, `mfcadpp.py`):
- **Split surfaces stay one surface.** A wall a crossing cut divided counts once (`wall_count` = distinct planes,
  `_distinct_planes`). A bare plane on the plane of a feature's face, inside the part's envelope, joins that
  feature when the two meet or both border the cut between them (`split_face_ids`).
- **Passages.** Coplanar wall pieces lying one after the other along the axis are one wall (a crossing cut), even
  when the gap opens outward; a T-slot's ledges lie side by side, so they stay apart. Overlapping passages leave a
  section with convex corners: a ring whose concave corners outnumber its convex ones is a hole's section (round
  a part's outside it is the reverse). An open chain of floorless walls whose two ends open into a through cut on
  the same axis (a bore, a passage) is what is left of a passage.
- **Floors.** Only the floor's outer loop can be open (`FaceInfo.outer_edge_ids`): a passage or hole through the
  floor, or an island on it, is an inner loop. Rounded ends and corners stand on the floor, never on a wall, and
  they bound the floor as flat walls do (a pocket at an angle to the axes).
- **Slots and steps.** A slot's side walls face each other; two walls facing the same way are a ridge or a
  staircase. A pocket's walls surround its floor (no half-plane holds their normals); a step's stand along one
  side. A step is cut at the outside of the part: a recess whose open sides lead only into other cuts is a pocket
  they broke into (except two walls square to each other, a corner step). The outside is a plane on the envelope,
  from the exact bounding box (`_envelope`, not the faces' tolerance-grown boxes) within 0.01 % of the part's size.
- **Round things.** Pieces of one cylinder count by the angle they cover together (`_angular_cover`), not by their
  summed sweeps: two half-cylinders on the same side are half a bore. A channel's cylinder is its floor, so a
  cylinder standing on a floor is never a channel. More than half a turn standing on a floor is a hole another cut
  opened (`OpenedBoreRecognizer`), or an O-ring groove if the floor also meets an inner pin. A convex corner that
  sits between two of a recess's walls (engraved lettering) is a wall of that recess (`convex_corner_face_ids`).
- **Chamfers.** A chamfer never has a concave edge along its run. It may end on a face across an inside corner
  (an edge that ends at a step floor), but only when narrower than 1:20 of the faces it breaks; wider, it is a
  triangular step's wall. An internal cone is a countersink only on a bore on its axis; otherwise it is the corner
  of a rim chamfer. A hole's entry cone is listed in `countersink_face_ids` (the dataset calls it a chamfer).

**Competing readings** (`FeatureCandidate.alternatives`, `ambiguous`). Two sources:
- **Near-threshold calls (`NEAR_THRESHOLD`):** a recognizer whose decision sat close to its own threshold reports
  the other reading. Examples: slot vs pocket within ±20 % of aspect 3, centre hole vs countersink within 2° of
  55°/65°, channel vs fillet within 20° of a 120° sweep.
- **Rival readings (`engine.add_rival_readings`):** each recognizer is re-run with nothing claimed. A different-type
  reading of mostly the same faces (overlap ≥ 0.6) is recorded. One that also takes another feature's faces is a
  different decomposition, and is dropped.

`ambiguous` means an alternative's confidence is within 0.1 of the feature's own. Review flags it, and
`POST /features/{id}/choose` takes a reading the recognition offered (never one the client makes up).

A recognizer must not rely on run order for its own correctness. `ChannelRecognizer` used to read closed bores as
channels when HoleRecognizer hadn't claimed them first; the shadow run exposed it.

Tests: `tests/test_brep_extraction.py` (closed-form primitives, mesh normals against exact normals, triangle → face
mapping). Ambiguity: `tests/test_ambiguity.py`.

**Mesh transport.** `GET /{id}/mesh.bin` (`tessellator.pack_mesh`, `api.parseMesh`) is little-endian: magic PMIM,
version, a JSON header, then 4-byte aligned f32/u32 buffers the browser maps as typed arrays. CTC-02 drops from
3.2 MB of JSON to 0.9 MB. `GET /mesh` stays for other clients. `GZipMiddleware` compresses every response over 2 kB.
`MeshData` arrays may be typed arrays: build indices with `THREE.Uint32BufferAttribute`, not `setIndex(number[])`.
Tests: `tests/test_mesh_binary.py`.

### PMI extraction (two readers merged)

1. **XCAF path** (`pmi_xcaf.py`) — `STEPCAFControl_Reader` with `SetGDTMode(True)`; `XCAFDoc_DimTolTool`
   gives dimensions, geometric tolerances, and datums with their referenced sub-shape labels, mapped to
   face ids in the same `TopExp_Explorer` order the tessellator uses.
2. **Part-21 text path** (`pmi_part21.py`) — a hand-written ISO 10303-21 tokenizer that walks the raw STEP
   text for dimension/tolerance/datum entities and resolves faces by matching `ADVANCED_FACE` analytic
   surfaces against the OCCT faces. It *always* runs: it fills in what XCAF drops, and is the sole source
   when XCAF returns nothing (PMI not linked to geometry).
3. `pmi_extractor.py` merges both, numbers characteristics (see balloon-number preservation below), and
   hands off to `balloon_placer.py` for anchor/label placement (face centroid + outward normal; unlinked
   PMI fans out along the bounding-box top edge and is flagged in `warnings`).

Characteristics carry `xcaf_index` when they came from XCAF (this is what makes them editable in place —
see `pmi_writer.py`); Part-21-only characteristics have no such label and can't be edited, only replaced.

**Datums.** XCAF often reads a datum's letter but not its faces, so `pmi_extractor._merge_datums` gives such a
datum the text reader's faces. The text reader follows GEOMETRIC_ITEM_SPECIFIC_USAGE and its supertype
ITEM_IDENTIFIED_REPRESENTATION_USAGE, nested composites, and datum targets. A target is linked through
FEATURE_FOR_DATUM_TARGET_RELATIONSHIP, or found from where it is drawn: a placed target's 'orientation'
placement, or a point, line or circle. Those points are matched to the faces they lie on (`_faces_at`). All
117 datums on the 19 NIST parts have faces (before this, 0 of 10 on FTC-06).

**Units.** A characteristic keeps the drawing's units (`unit`: `in`, `mm`, `deg`), and geometry is always mm.
The text reader resolves each measure's own unit from its conversion factor, not its name, and its values
are the ones kept. XCAF converts some measures to mm and leaves others as written, and can pair an inch
nominal with a mm deviation (NIST FTC-06). So `pmi_extractor` pairs the two readers' items in this order:
1. value up to a unit factor
2. file order
3. same faces
4. tolerances XCAF read without a value

When several text items fit one XCAF item (two dimensions with the same nominal), the one with the same
limits is taken. It then adopts the text reader's values. XCAF-only items are converted with the factors
learnt from those pairs. The text reader can put two coaxial cylinders of one radius on the same face, so
never pair on faces alone. Anything that compares PMI with geometry uses `ModelRecord.characteristics_mm`
(`pmi_types.in_model_units`). PMI add/edit is refused on a drawing whose lengths aren't in mm, because OCCT's
writer would rewrite every inch value as mm. Tests: `tests/test_pmi_units.py`.

### PMI authoring (writing back into STEP)

`pmi_writer.py` extends `scripts/_pmi_helpers.py`'s `PmiBuilder` technique — originally "build a fresh XCAF
document from a bare shape" (used by the `make_sample*.py` generators) — to "extend the document
`step_loader.load_step` already read from a real file," so everything already in the file survives. Known
gaps (documented in that module, worth checking before touching this code):
* A brand-new datum can't be authored in the same pass (OCCT mis-resolves its identity on the next
  round-trip) — `datum_refs` may only name a datum already present on the model.
* An edge/vertex-referenced characteristic doesn't get a visual anchor yet — `pmi_xcaf._ref_face_ids` only
  resolves *face* references into the balloon-anchoring `face_ids` list.
* A geometric tolerance's semantic name gets mangled unpredictably by `STEPCAFControl_Writer`.

**A write rewrites the whole file**, so it must leave every other characteristic as it was. OCCT's reader
and writer disagree with each other on three points, and `pmi_writer` corrects each one:

* `_repair_document` runs when a file is opened for writing. OCCT's reader loads a datum-referenced
  angularity, concentricity, coaxiality or circular run-out as a position tolerance, and gives an angle in
  degrees that its writer then tags as radians. The repair puts the type and the angle back from the merged
  extraction (matched by `xcaf_index`). Without it, any write turned those four into Position and 90° into
  5156.6°.
* `_set_plus_minus` stores the lower deviation negated, because OCCT keeps it as a magnitude and writes its
  negative. `scripts/_pmi_helpers.py` does the same; keep the two in step.
* An angular dimension's nominal and deviations all go in as radians.

After the write, `routers/models.py`'s `_write_and_reingest` compares the characteristics it was not asked to
change (`model_checks.pmi_damaged_by_write`) and refuses the write if any changed or vanished. This is what
happens on most real files: of the 6 NIST parts with mm PMI, OCCT reports write errors on 5 and silently drops
or merges tolerances on the other. Authoring is reliable on files this app wrote, not yet on files from
other CAD systems. Writing only the new entities into the original text is the way out. Face ids may change across
a rewrite (a face id is only a traversal index), so nothing durable keys on one. Tests: `tests/test_pmi_roundtrip.py`.

**Hole ends.** A hole's `through` / `bottom_type` comes from what closes each end of its coaxial stack
(`_HoleStack` in `recognizers.py`). A flat face that faces back into the hole is a floor. A cone that narrows
to a point is the drill point: the hole is blind, `depth` stops at the end of the full diameter and
`tip_depth` reaches the tip. Only a cone that widens onto the entry is a countersink. `line_of_sight` is one
ray plus one point-in-solid test per stretch between crossings; keep it that way (sampling every millimetre
took 37 s on NIST CTC-04).

### Balloon-number preservation

When PMI is transcribed from a legacy 2D drawing, naming a dimension/tolerance with its original balloon
number (`"5"`, `"C5"`, `"5 - bore dia"`) makes the app use that number as the characteristic id instead of
auto-numbering, stripping the number from the displayed feature name. Implementation:
`pmi_extractor._assign_numbers` / `_DRAWING_NUMBER_RE`; see its tests for the exact matching rules (a bare
`"6 mm hole"` is deliberately *not* treated as a balloon number).

### 2D drawing (`GET /api/models/{id}/drawing`, `.svg`, `.dxf`)

`services/drawing.py`. Views come from OpenCASCADE hidden-line removal (`HLRBRep_Algo`) on the solid, in third angle
(top above front, right beside it) plus an isometric. Visible edges solid, hidden dashed, tangent edges thin. Each
characteristic's balloon goes in the view that looks most squarely at its anchor's face, outside the view's outline, with a
leader to the anchor; balloons are relaxed so none overlap. Datums get a boxed letter. Overall sizes are measured from
the geometry, drawn grey and labelled as not PMI. The table lists each characteristic's requirement exactly as the file
states it. Sheet coordinates are mm with y up (DXF convention); `to_svg` flips y. The sheet widens by one table column
block per 36 characteristics. Tests: `tests/test_drawing.py`.

### Frontend

Single-view app (`App.tsx` holds top-level state), no router, no test suite. `api.ts` wraps `/api/models*`.
`types.ts` is a hand-maintained mirror of the backend's JSON: keep it in sync when a response changes.
`components/studio/Studio.tsx` is the dark studio shell: top bar with the **GD&T / Features / Drawing** workspace switch,
a contextual toolbar, the dock panel, the 3D view (or the drawing) and a status bar. Colours are `--st-*` tokens in
`index.css`. Icons are lucide line icons behind `studio/icons.tsx`.

* **GD&T**: `PmiManager.tsx` (datums, characteristics, properties) and `PmiModal.tsx` (add/edit, docked). `Viewer3D.tsx`
  owns the three.js scene, picking, balloons and the axis-snapped views (a narrowed perspective FOV, not an orthographic
  camera). `highlightFaces`, `focusFaces`, `onFaceClick` and `hidePmi` let a workspace drive it.
* **Features**: `features/useFeatures.ts` + `FeaturesPanel.tsx`: the recognized features, their size, confidence, evidence and
  alternatives. A row or a face click selects a feature and highlights its faces.
* **Drawing**: `drawing/DrawingView.tsx` (the SVG, with zoom) and `DrawingPanel.tsx` (balloon list, SVG/DXF downloads).
